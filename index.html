How it works

A symmetry splits the plane into orbits: sets of points that must share one colour. Orbipix colours each orbit of the pattern with a weighted average of the image over the same orbit, with weights from a mask.

There are two kinds of mask. A height map (the default) stands over the image: each orbit shows its highest copies, and level ones are averaged, so the flat start averages every copy. Brushing raises or lowers it, and it stays with the image when the symmetry changes.

A contravariant mask starts as the fundamental domain: weight 1 inside, 0 outside, so you get the usual tiling. Feather softens its edges, so neighbouring copies blend instead of meeting at a seam.

The mask is contravariant: the weights over each orbit add up to one. Adjust image lays the image over the pattern where it lies, faint where its weight is low. There, Brush raises the weight under it and lowers the rest of each orbit to match, pulling that part of the image into the pattern, wherever it is, in as many pieces as you like. Erase does the opposite.

It is redrawn from the domain whenever the signature changes.
